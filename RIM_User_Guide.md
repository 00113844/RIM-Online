What is RIM?RIM or Ryegrass Integrated Management is an Excel-based software developed by the University of Western Australia.Intended as a learning and extension tool for agronomists, farmers and students, RIM allows users to experiment with a number of crop rotations and weed management options, to build strategies and test their performance. RIM's main outputs are financial gross margins and ryegrass plants and seeds numbers per hectare.The modelling underlying RIM includes hundreds of parameters built around data and assumptions. This guide clarifies a number of questions, including the main assumptions behind the options and where parameters can be customised. Results are per hectare or per $\text{m}^2$.1. Seven Time Periods in a YearManagement options occur at specific times. Each period tracks the resulting changes in ryegrass seeds and plant numbers over the year:Prior to break0–10 days after break10–20 days after breakBefore post-emergence sprayEarly springBefore harvestSummer2. The FinancesAt the end of the year, gross margins are calculated from the receipts (yield $\times$ prices) and from the cost of seeding, harvesting and weed control. A long-term average (or "nominal annuity") accounts for tax, inflation and interest.3. The Seed BankA proportion of seeds remains dormant in the soil seed bank, another cohort dies (natural mortality), while the rest germinates in cohorts at different times of the year following a specific pattern. Germination progressively depletes the seed bank which is replenished in spring, when new seeds are produced by mature ryegrass plants that survived the growing season.4. Ryegrass PlantsThe population of each period is comprised of newly germinated plants and the survivors from the previous period. Ultimately, the population of ryegrass in early spring will determine the effect of the weed burden on crop yields (competition). The age of the weeds and the crop density will impact the number of seeds produced by surviving ryegrass.Timing & Seeding Controls: Tickle / Plough, Knock-down, Double-knock, Pre-Em. (on germination), No-till or full-cut seeding, Post-Em., Spring options, Harvest options.Plant Dynamics Flow: Seed Bank $\rightarrow$ Germination % (Dry, Wet, Delayed 1–2 weeks / 2–3 weeks) $\rightarrow$ Ryegrass Plants $\rightarrow$ Mature Survivors $\rightarrow$ New Seeds to Seed Bank.5. Agronomic FactorsRotations, seeding technique and timing, grazing intensity, crop sacrifice, spraying or swathing all have positive or negative impacts on both yields and finances.
